ESP32-P4 Hub Keylogger — unified flasher

Ports of QWavey’s esp32-p4-hub-keylogger for the Spotpear ESP32-P4-MINI-B-M-EXT (P4 + on-board C6). One page — flash both chips right from your browser.
Order matters. Flash the P4 first, then the C6. The U4 switch on the M-EXT baseboard multiplexes the USB port — the wrong combination will silently fail.

Requirements

Chromium browser (Chrome, Edge, Opera, Brave, Arc), a USB-C data cable, and a Spotpear ESP32-P4-MINI-B-M-EXT (N32R32 variant so the 16 MB MSC partition fits). Nothing is uploaded — images are served from this page and written straight to the chip in your browser.

2

Prepare the P4

Route the Type-C to the ESP32-P4’s USB-Serial-JTAG and hold it in the ROM boot loader.

  • Move the U4 switch to position 0 (P4 side).
  • Hold BOOT on the P4-MINI, tap RESET, then release BOOT.
  • Your PC enumerates a new serial port — that’s the P4 ROM loader.
3

Flash the P4 firmware chip family: ESP32-P4

Click Connect & flash P4 and pick the port that just appeared. About 15 seconds. When it’s done, tap RESET on the P4 (no BOOT this time).

Prepare the C6

Route the same Type-C to the on-board ESP32-C6-MINI instead, then hold it in ROM.

  • Move the U4 switch to position 1 (C6 side).
  • Hold C6_BOOT on the M-EXT board, tap C6_RESET, release C6_BOOT.
  • Another fresh serial port appears — the C6 in ROM.
5

Flash the C6 firmware chip family: ESP32-C6

Click Connect & flash C6 and pick the C6’s new port. About 30 seconds — the C6 image is bigger (2 MB SPIFFS bundle with the full Hak5 language pack).

Connect

Flip the U4 switch back to 0 (P4 side) and reset. The P4 is what the target PC sees over USB (HID + MSC composite). The C6 sits on-board and serves the Wi-Fi AP.

  • Look for the Wi-Fi network P4-KeyLogger — password changeme123.
  • Open http://192.168.4.1 — that’s the ported BadUSB UI.
